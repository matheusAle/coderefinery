# What makes a developer-tool landing page convincing to experienced developers (2025–2026), and what they dismiss as marketing

Scope note: evidence is drawn from primary HN threads (via hn.algolia.com), GitHub discussions, practitioner posts (Evil Martians, PostHog, Instruqt, daily.dev), and the 2025 Stack Overflow survey. 2023–2024 items are labelled. Every quote is verbatim from the linked source. Where a search returned nothing reliable, it is listed under Gaps rather than guessed.

## Key Question 1: What do developers on HN/Reddit say convinced them to try a tool, in their own words?

### Takeaway
Developers describe being convinced by (a) their own hands-on run of the tool ("I tested it on my repo"), (b) prior trust in the same team's earlier tool (Ruff → uv), and (c) zero-friction trial (no sign-up, local, free). The recurring dismissal is "what does it actually do?" when the page leads with abstract nouns ("manage", "AI-powered") instead of a concrete mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- On uv's launch (HN, Feb 2024, 647 points, 210 comments — labelled 2024), the strongest "convinced" signal was transferred trust from Ruff: drcongo wrote "I've been dying to see what they came up with next given how in love I am with Ruff." and ericra wrote "While I don't see the immediate need to switch from using pip, I am such a fan of ruff" — [HN: Uv: Python packaging in Rust](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39387641)
- In the same thread, credibility was established by readers running it themselves, not by the announcement: driverdan: "I did some basic testing today and uv was around 2x faster than pip for a clean venv and cold cache" and "With warm uv cache it was incredibly fast, under 10 sec."; lijok: "I tested it on my repo, multiple errors, it's not a drop-in replacement." — [HN: Uv: Python packaging in Rust](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39387641)
- VC-funding skepticism was a recurring counter-signal: hprotagonist: "A VC-backed pip-and-more doesn't make sense to me."; another commenter: "Astral being transparent on how they plan to make money would be very helpful." — [HN: Uv: Python packaging in Rust](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39387641)
- Bun 1.0 (HN, Sept 2023, 150 points — labelled 2023): what convinced people was hands-on feel, not claims: trebor: "I played with it for several hours, and was impressed by how snappy it feels."; leejoramo: "I could see myself migrating to it within a year if Bun's performance and developer usability claims are justified" — [HN: Bun 1.0 announcement](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37422106)
- Zed 1.0 (HN, 29 Apr 2026, 2,153 points, 690 comments): NortySpock said Zed was easy to start with because it had "zero sign-up friction" for connecting a local Ollama model; conversely mfontani: "Why does signing up through Github require the "act on behalf" permission?" … "That seems risky."; daemin asked "With Zed touting itself as an AI first editor, is it possible to completely disable all AI features" and nacs replied "it does have a very prominent button to completely disable all AI." — [HN: Zed 1.0](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47949027)
- Zed 1.0: performance was judged by readers' own measurements rather than the post's claims — nh2: "I tried Zed last month but found that it uses high CPU usage even when idle (up to 50% of 1 core of my i7-7500U)." and "Zed spams syscalls even when its screen is entirely still:" — [HN: Zed 1.0](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47949027)
- The dominant HN dismissal pattern in 2025–2026 is the literal phrase "what does it actually do?", directed at pages that use abstract verbs: _1tem on a Show HN (Sept 2025): "The readme keeps using the word "manage" but what does it actually do?" — [HN comment 45156636](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45156636); quodlibet on Show HN: Whisper Money (Dec 2025): "Looks promising, but what does it actually do?" — [HN comment 46417506](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46417506); dotancohen on Pocketbase (Nov 2025): "What does it actually do? Yes, I know what a backend is" — [HN comment 46076409](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46076409); dkdbejwi383 on iTerm 2 Companion App (Sept 2026): "The page just states "it lets your phone work together with your terminal" but what does that actually mean?" — [HN comment 49643799](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49643799); rovr138 on Show HN: Winslop (Feb 2026): "So what does it actually do?" — [HN comment 47106679](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47106679)
- a96 (Sept 2025) on marketing-flavoured docs: "Instead you get something that looks like marketing copy written by LLM fed on BS generator output." — [HN comment 45330396](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45330396)
- Show HN "AI-powered" products get the comparison question immediately: insiderprint on "Show HN: AI Resume Reviewer Built with Next.js" (Oct 2026): "good ...How it is different than all other AI powered parsers?" — [HN comment 50274472](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50274472)
- Warp (Nov 2024, labelled 2024): the mandatory-login requirement was lifted "after taking in requests from hundreds of developers who didn't like the login requirement for Warp"; default-on telemetry remained the stated reason some developers refuse it — [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/news/warp-terminal-no-login/); [OMG! Ubuntu](https://www.omgubuntu.co.uk/2024/11/warp-ai-terminal-login-change)
- Show HN launch postmortem (CodeYam, March 2026): the team followed the Show HN guidance to make the product "free, local, and usable without sign-up", recorded two demo videos, and redesigned the site to "emphasize how easy it is to get started" — yet "it has become extremely difficult for a launch to gain any visibility without getting very lucky." — [Nadia Eldeib, What I learned launching CodeYam](https://blog.nseldeib.com/p/what-i-learned-launching-codeyam)
- Instruqt (Apr 2026, vendor): "developers trust other developers more than they'll ever trust your brand voice." and developers "assume every claim is an overclaim until proven otherwise." — [Instruqt: Why developers don't trust marketing](https://instruqt.com/blog/why-developers-dont-trust-marketing-and-what-you-can-do-about-it)
- PostHog's own rules (newsletter): "If you wouldn't be proud to share your marketing with a friend, you shouldn't do it."; avoid "tedious, clickbaity, hyperbolic marketing tactics" — "It's patronizing."; "Focusing on benefits rather than features is tired."; "Speak like your users."; on HN: "don't try to game it" and "you'll have a 1 in 10 hit rate." — [PostHog: 40 things we've learned about marketing for developers](https://posthog.com/newsletter/marketing-for-devs)

### Inferences
- "Convinced" almost never means "the landing page convinced me." It means "the page got out of the way fast enough for me to run it, and the run matched the claim." The page's job is to make the first run take under a minute and to make the claim specific enough to be falsifiable.
- Team reputation (Astral/Ruff, Zed/Atom founders) is a stronger prior than any copy; a page for an unknown team should lean on a reproducible artifact instead.
- A page that cannot survive the question "what does it actually do?" in the first sentence will get that exact question as the top comment.

### Gaps
- No Reddit thread found that directly asks developers "what convinced you to try a tool"; Reddit snapshots surfaced were off-topic (an r/EntrepreneurRideAlong post). Reddit evidence here is thin.
- No reliable Gortex-specific HN/Reddit reaction found; only the repo README and install domain (get.gortex.dev) surfaced, with no launch thread.

## Key Question 2: How do developers react to benchmark claims (e.g. "3× faster"), and what makes them believe them?

### Takeaway
Big-ratio claims trigger immediate disbelief ("so large I can't believe them"); the credibility threshold is a public, runnable benchmark repo with stated hardware, like-for-like configuration (same transpiler, same cache state), and honest rounding. The canonical cautionary case is Vercel's Turbopack "10x faster than Vite" (Oct–Nov 2022), which Evan You reduced to under 2x and which Vercel had to correct by publishing its benchmark code.

### Cited Findings
- Vercel claimed Turbopack was "10x faster than Vite" in its tweet, blog and emails without linking benchmarks. Evan You (Vite author) rebuilt the benchmark and found Vite used Babel while Turbopack used SWC — "This alone makes the benchmark a fundamentally unfair comparison."; the 15ms vs 87ms figures were displayed as 0.01s vs 0.09s ("closer to 6x"); and the 10x gap only appeared above 30k modules — "Using the 30k number to justify the 10x claim feels like cherry-picking." He characterised it as "aggressive marketing using cherry-picked, non-peer-reviewed, borderline-misleading numbers". Vercel then published its methodology and benchmark code, which Evan called "a step in the right direction" that "should have been done on day one." (Nov 2022 — labelled) — [GitHub discussion: yyx990803/vite-vs-next-turbo-hmr #8](https://github.com/yyx990803/vite-vs-next-turbo-hmr/discussions/8)
- HN reaction to Vercel's follow-up "Turbopack Performance Benchmarks" post (Nov 2022, 99 points — labelled): Bilal_io: "But is it realistic to boast about unrealistic scenarios? It erks me." and "The 700x speed gain compared to webpack is for dev mode changing 30000 files at once.."; zoover2020: "At this level of detail it _is_ super misleading however to say "a 10x improvement" whilst in reality it's more like 6x"; rk06: "Like why not use the actual verifiable "apple to apple" benchmark results?"; tough: "Marketing always overrides engineering on final copy"; pyrolistical (on the correction): "Good on them make a correction" — [HN: Turbopack Performance Benchmarks](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=33415371)
- Bun 1.0 (2023 — labelled): llwj: "The performance differences are so large (5x, 6x, and 18x faster) that I can't believe them."; christophilus replied "I found it to be about 2x as fast on a real world project" … "I'm sure it varies quite a bit based on the project internals." — [HN: Bun 1.0 announcement](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37422106)
- Bun benchmark criticism elsewhere: a Lemmy commenter said the benchmarks "seem to be very cherry picked, especially the testing framework part, where bun compares its speed to one of the slowest testing frameworks out there (jest)" — [Lemmy thread](https://lemmy.nekusoul.de/post/73101); a DEV commenter called the claim "Bun's JavaScriptCore outperforms V8 in HTTP throughput" "controversial and somewhat misleading" — [DEV comment](https://dev.to/jaood_xd/comment/2ojn3)
- 2025 Show HN example of the demand for reproducibility: djray (June 2025) on "Show HN: I am making an app to rival 'Everything'": "I can't believe this is faster than a comparable Windows 11 search, as you claim on your GitHub" … "I suspect the Windows search was for a non-indexed location and/or from a cold start" … "if you're going to make claims like this, you should publish reproducible benchmarks" "so people can test your methodology." — [HN comment 44238100](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44238100)
- HN on the Benchmarks Game (May 2026): pron: "A website that doesn't give people the relevant context for the data it offers is not a good website" and "The website should at least explain just how skewed the results are in favour of conditions"; igouy: "comparison of "language speeds" is so under-specified as-to-be wishful thinking." — [HN thread 47822414](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47822414)
- Biome's "35x faster than Prettier" headline is not what its own repo says: the README (M1 MacBook Pro) reports "~25 times faster than Prettier", and "100 times faster than Prettier on an M1 Max with 10 cores", with the caveat that "the speed-ups for the multithreaded benchmarks can vary significantly depending on the setup"; it also discloses that it "does not measure incremental formatting as it measures cold formatting time" and limits Prettier to JS/TS files — [biomejs/benchmark](https://github.com/biomejs/benchmark). No independent reproduction or formal critique of the 35x figure was found.
- Nx's counter-benchmark against Turborepo is published as a runnable repo (`npm run benchmark`) with stated hardware (M2 Max, macOS 14, 12 Feb 2025) and invites PRs: "If it happens that the repo surfaces some edge case with how turbo works, send a PR, and let's fix it." Reported: Nx 191.7 vs Turbo 1432.4 vs Lage 1258.6 (ms avg), "Nx is 6.57x faster than Lage and 7.47x faster than Turbo." It also concedes "the cache restoration Turborepo provides is likely to be fast enough for a lot of small and mid-size repos" — [vsavkin/large-monorepo](https://github.com/vsavkin/large-monorepo)
- Vercel later corrected a docs claim about Nx: commit "docs: [correction] nx remote caching is free like Turborepo (#10460)" removed a statement the commit message says "was never accurate in Nx" — [vercel/turbo commit 13f3210](https://app.semanticdiff.com/gh/vercel/turbo/commit/13f32101246291cdc243bc424b41dd49b94ead22)
- Theo (t3.gg) on a viral SSR benchmark (Apr 2024 — labelled): adding a 100 ms data delay narrowed a ~14x gap to ~1.5x; "the more you make this resemble the real world the less these benchmarks matter"; Ryan Carniato: "benchmarking is easy unintentionally cheating benchmarks is easier" — [t3dotgg: We need to talk about this benchmark](https://rosetta.to/u/t3dotgg/we-need-to-talk-about-this-benchmark)
- Practitioner guidance (Evil Martians, Mar 2025): replace "Blazing fast" with a specific, bounded claim like a JPEG server "at least 35% faster than alternatives"; Evil Martians reports its own conversion rose from 0.1% to 2.0% after replacing vague language with specific metrics and testing CTAs — [Evil Martians: How to kill conversions](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/how-to-kill-conversions-on-your-developer-tool-landing-page)
- uv thread: the only benchmark comments that landed were readers' own with stated conditions ("clean venv and cold cache"); the post's "10-100x" number itself was not debated in the fetched portion — [HN: Uv: Python packaging in Rust](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39387641)

### Inferences
- The believable shape of a speed claim is: modest ratio (2–7x, not 10–700x), stated hardware, cold vs warm cache distinguished, like-for-like configuration named, and a repo link in the same sentence. "At least 35% faster" with a method reads as more credible than "10x" without one.
- Corrections are rewarded ("Good on them make a correction"), but the correction becomes the story; publishing the method first avoids that.
- Readers assume marketing rounded up. Showing unrounded ms values and the comparison's worst case preempts the "0.01s vs 0.09s" complaint.

### Gaps
- No HN thread specifically about Turborepo-vs-Nx benchmarks on turbo.build was found; the documented controversy is Turbopack-vs-Vite (2022). The Nx dispute is documented only via the Nx-side repo and a Vercel docs correction.
- No independent critique of Astral's "10-100x" figure for uv was found; reactions were anecdotal.
- No A/B data on benchmark presentation (e.g. chart vs table vs repo link) was found from any practitioner.

## Key Question 3: Is a long scroll-driven animated explainer (Apple-style) respected or resented by developers?

### Takeaway
Direct 2025–2026 evidence is thin but uniformly negative where it exists; the clearest counter-signal is PlanetScale deliberately exiting the "razzle dazzle" marketing-site style for a man-page look (Oct 2024) and the CEO calling the swap unanimous, while a vocal minority of developers (HN, Dec 2025) hated the result. Evil Martians' 100-page study found most dev-tool heroes use static or lightly animated UI, with scroll-driven storytelling not among the recommended patterns.

### Cited Findings
- PlanetScale's designer Jason Long on the move to plaintext: "It was our CEO's decision to see if it was feasible to do a plain text design that felt like a man page."; "We felt like we could exit the hype cycle of feeling pressure to design a marketing site"; "We wanted to let our tech and customer logos do the talking rather than razzle dazzle."; "Putting out a dirt simple site let's us focus our limited resources (I'm the only designer in the company) on our product."; "We started with some very minimal designs but they felt like they were in the uncanny valley."; the change "coincided with discontinuing our free plan" and a decision "to focus on our larger customers all the way up to enormous enterprises" who "want to know their database company is serious, rock solid" — [Anthony Hobday interview with Jason Long](https://anthonyhobday.com/sideprojects/interviews/jason-long.html)
- HN reaction at launch (31 Oct 2024, 17 points, 4 comments — labelled 2024): fosterfriends: "I love seeing a company lean into something faster and simpler for their marketing." … "I'm sure this was a fist fight internally to approve, but I'm here for it"; PlanetScale CEO samlambert: "no fights at all. we all wanted to do it."; zoezoezoezoe: "Maybe I'm too linear pilled (I am), but I really liked their old landing page," … "their new landing page feels like early web 1.0 stuff" — [HN: PlanetScale Updated to Plaintext Website](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=42006874)
- HN reaction a year later (PlanetScale Metal GA thread, 15 Dec 2025): wackget: "If anyone from Planetscale is reading this, please know I hate what you did to your website."; HatchedLake721: "I struggle reading white text on black backgrounds, so I don't even try to read their product pages"; mesmertech: "not a fan of the fake hacker "we don't do CSS or styling""; dodomodo: "I have a hard time understanding the layout of the website on mobile"; samdoesnothing: "Design is subjective of course. I love their new website and much prefer it to the old one."; samlambert: "Thank you for the feedback." — [HN: $50 PlanetScale Metal Is GA for Postgres](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=46276378)
- The only 2025 HN comment found reacting to a scroll-animated product page (Material 3 Expressive, May 2025, not a dev tool): PennRobotics: "Instead, the generally useless, overly-padded animation takes the majority of the space." … "two-column images are misaligned vertically because of the scroll animation" … "fading in elements. WHY??" — [HN comment 44028031](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=44028031)
- Bun 1.0 (2023 — labelled) on video-first explainers: RetroTechie: "I am not a fan of video to explain stuff that a bit of text does better"; stephc_int13: "I think the announcement video was a bit forced/awkward"; Bun's colinmcd: "This video is definitely corny. (I'm in it.)"; graypegg (positive): "I like this direct format of video changelog." — [HN: Bun 1.0 announcement](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=37422106)
- Evil Martians' review of 100+ dev-tool landing pages (July 2025) recommends choosing the hero visual by maturity — "animated or static UI, a live embed for narrow tools, a code snippet for libraries/SDKs, or an abstract graphic for pre-launch" — names Linear (static UI), Tailwind (code snippet) and Pixelcut (live demo) as patterns, and summarises the tone as "No salesy BS." and "Clever and simple wins." It found "only a handful" show pricing and comparison tables are "rarely used." — [Evil Martians: We studied 100 dev tool landing pages](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/we-studied-100-devtool-landing-pages-here-is-what-actually-works-in-2025)
- daily.dev's guide (updated May 2026, secondary): warns that if visitors must "scroll more than 1,000 pixels to find a syntax-highlighted code block" the page risks losing up to 50% of technical traffic (figure unsourced), and that "Trusted By" logos placed before code or product visuals may cause developers to leave — [daily.dev: Developer-first landing pages](https://business.daily.dev/resources/create-developer-first-landing-pages-convert/)
- Linear: a Product Hunt thread on Linear's new homepage had commenters noting a working demo on the page is a plus over static screenshots — [Product Hunt: Linear's new homepage](https://www.producthunt.com/p/linear/what-do-you-think-of-linear-s-new-homepage). No HN thread on Linear's homepage was found.

### Inferences
- Among the sources found, nobody on HN praised a scroll-driven explainer for a developer tool; the praise went to "faster and simpler" and to pages where a code block or demo appears before any animation. The PlanetScale backlash was about legibility (white-on-black, mobile layout, "fake hacker" affect), not about missing animation — so the lesson is "terse and readable", not "ugly on purpose".
- Scroll-driven walkthroughs are tolerated when they can be skipped and when a static code/CLI artifact is reachable above the fold; a page that gates "what it does" behind scrolling will draw the "what does it actually do?" comment.

### Gaps
- No HN or Reddit thread found that evaluates a specific Apple-style scroll explainer on a developer-tool site (Vercel, Cursor, Warp) in 2025–2026; the brief's premise could not be directly tested.
- No conversion data comparing animated vs static hero sections for developer audiences was found.

## Key Question 4: Do side-by-side "problem → fix" code demos work better than stating the pain in prose?

### Takeaway
Practitioner sources converge on "show the code/terminal before the prose" and on specific, falsifiable claims; the strongest quantitative datapoint is Evil Martians' self-reported 0.1% → 2.0% conversion lift from replacing vague language with specific metrics. No controlled test of before/after code vs prose was found.

### Cited Findings
- Evil Martians (Mar 2025): "Mistake 1: maintain vague language" — vague pitch made visitors think the firm was an incubator or OSS collective; replacing "We understand developers" with "We work with 30 VC-backed early stage developer tools startups each year" and "Blazing fast" with a bounded "at least 35% faster" claim; conversion rose from 0.1% to 2.0%. Also: changing CTA copy "Build with us" → "Hire Martians" raised clicks from 1.3% to 2.0%; a direct Cal.com booking converted at 2% vs contact forms peaking at 0.1%; a crowded first screen (social proof, newsletter, translations, social buttons) was removed after user interviews showed developers "got lost before reaching basic information." — [Evil Martians: How to kill conversions](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/how-to-kill-conversions-on-your-developer-tool-landing-page)
- Evil Martians (July 2025): "Highlight real user problems and the way the product solves them instead of talking about features."; Tailwind cited as the code-snippet hero pattern; Pixelcut as the live-demo pattern — [Evil Martians: 100 landing pages](https://evilmartians.com/chronicles/we-studied-100-devtool-landing-pages-here-is-what-actually-works-in-2025)
- daily.dev (May 2026, secondary): a developer landing page "thrives on four key elements" — hero, working code sample, social proof from other developers, clear CTA; sample code should use realistic values rather than `foo`; CLI pages should show an install command with a copy button plus a GIF or terminal emulator; Avery Carter: "For technical audiences, proof beats polish."; it also claims 90% of B2D companies use abstract visuals or stock photos instead of the product — [daily.dev: Developer-first landing pages](https://business.daily.dev/resources/create-developer-first-landing-pages-convert/)
- Instruqt (Apr 2026, vendor): a developer running a working example gathers evidence "worth orders of magnitude more" than reading a landing page; pages that name limitations or "When not to use this" build credibility; funnels built around polished pitches instead of hands-on interaction are "optimizing for the wrong moment." — [Instruqt](https://instruqt.com/blog/why-developers-dont-trust-marketing-and-what-you-can-do-about-it)
- HN (uv, 2024): spapas82 stated a concrete feature-parity blocker rather than reacting to prose: "I can't use it unless it implements list -o and install -U" — [HN: uv](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=39387641)
- PostHog's position against benefit-prose: "Focusing on benefits rather than features is tired." and "Treat your website like a product" … "your website is your best sales person and is key to getting people to self-serve successfully." — [PostHog newsletter](https://posthog.com/newsletter/marketing-for-devs)
- Forest Admin (older, Medium — labelled): suggested homepage elements for developers are "a working demo, code excerpts, and thorough documentation"; "generic marketing that tries to sell rather than genuinely present your product is said to fall flat" — [Forest Admin: 4 must-haves](https://medium.com/forest-admin/4-must-haves-to-convert-developers-on-your-homepage-e07d850f63f4)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports "specific over vague" and "artifact over prose" strongly; it supports "before/after diff" specifically only by analogy (the Evil Martians rewrite is itself a before→after of copy). A before/after code block is the most compact way to make a claim falsifiable, which is what the benchmark findings in Q2 say developers demand.
- For a CLI tool, the equivalent of before/after is a real terminal transcript with exit codes and timings; HN readers reproduce exactly that in comments (driverdan's cold/warm cache numbers), so putting it on the page pre-empts the thread.

### Gaps
- No A/B test comparing side-by-side code vs prose for developer landing pages was found from PostHog, Evil Martians, Draft.dev, Swizec or Kyle Gawley.
- Draft.dev: only a webinar recap with a heading "Overselling and Dishonesty" was found, no landing-page guidance — [Draft.dev: Standing out in saturated dev tool markets](https://draft.dev/learn/standing-out-in-saturated-dev-tool-markets)
- Swizec and Kyle Gawley: no relevant posts surfaced in searches.

## Key Question 5: Which copy patterns are mocked and which are praised?

### Takeaway
Mocked: "AI-powered" as the value prop, adjective-heavy claims ("seamless", "powerful", "Blazing fast"), unexplained big multipliers, "Book a Demo"/"Contact Sales" as the only CTA, and abstract verbs ("manage", "lets your phone work together with your terminal"). Praised: a first sentence that names the mechanism, bounded numbers with method, no sign-up, a prominent off-switch for AI features, and honest limitations. The 2025 Stack Overflow survey quantifies the AI-trust deficit the "AI-powered" label runs into.

### Cited Findings
- Stack Overflow 2025 Developer Survey: on trust in AI-tool accuracy, "Highly trust" 3.1%, "Somewhat trust" 29.6%, "Somewhat distrust" 26.1%, "Highly distrust" 19.6% (≈33% trust vs ≈46% distrust); experienced developers 2.5% highly trust / 20.7% highly distrust; 66% chose "AI solutions that are almost right, but not quite" as their top frustration; 45.2% "Debugging AI-generated code is more time-consuming" — [Stack Overflow 2025 survey: AI](https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai)
- HN sentiment toward "AI-powered" in late 2026 is dismissive-by-default: kenhwang: "The new AI powered Alexa beta basically can't do egg timer, music, or light switch duties" — [HN comment 50264815](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50264815); insiderprint on a Show HN: "How it is different than all other AI powered parsers?" — [HN comment 50274472](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50274472); Foxhuls on "the plethora of AI powered devices and pendants already existing and I constantly get served ads for." — [HN comment 50358085](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50358085)
- Zed 1.0: the "AI first editor" positioning triggered an opt-out question; the praised answer was a "very prominent button to completely disable all AI." — [HN: Zed 1.0](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47949027)
- Instruqt (Apr 2026): words like "seamless" and "powerful" "carry almost no signal," and heavy adjective use can suggest weak confidence in the product; developers compare notes on GitHub, HN and Reddit where "Spin doesn't survive that environment." — [Instruqt](https://instruqt.com/blog/why-developers-dont-trust-marketing-and-what-you-can-do-about-it)
- daily.dev (May 2026): flagged generic terms "Scalable," "Powerful," "Easy," "revolutionary," "best-in-class," "game-changing"; manager-oriented phrasing examples "Unlock the power of AI" and "Supercharge your workflow"; Filip Nakov: "If your first 3-4 headline words don't instantly explain what your product does" the page fails, and "Nobody wants to 'Book a Demo.'"; preferred CTAs: "View Docs," "Try the Playground," "See the Code," "Explore the Repo," "Install," "Fork on GitHub"; discouraged: "Book a Demo," "Get Started," "Learn More," "Contact Sales"; microcopy "No credit card required," "Works with GitHub" — [daily.dev](https://business.daily.dev/resources/create-developer-first-landing-pages-convert/)
- Newsletter on dev-focused GTM: developers "have a love/hate relationship with the 'Book a demo' call to action. Mostly hate though."; PostHog cited as the positive pattern (transparent pricing, recorded ungated demo, free plan, sales call only for custom needs) — [newslepear #14](https://newslepear.beehiiv.com/p/14-book-demo-flow-writing-content-devs-devfocused-gtm-playbook)
- A 2026 B2B SaaS statistics roundup (secondary, citing Topickz) reports dev tools are the most generous on free tiers (89%) and the most opaque on pricing (42% hidden) — [Programming Insider](https://programminginsider.com/b2b-saas-buyer-behavior-statistics-2026-free-to-cite/)

"Exit codes / honest limitations" were not found as explicit praise, but "When not to use this" sections are recommended by Instruqt and the Nx repo's concession that Turborepo is "fast enough for a lot of small and mid-size repos" is the pattern.

## Key Question 6 (supplementary): Documented reactions to specific sites and Show HN feedback patterns

### Takeaway
Only PlanetScale (two HN threads), Zed 1.0, Bun 1.0, uv, Turbopack and Warp have documented primary reactions to their presentation. Linear, Vercel (site), Cursor, Biome, Gortex and Astral's site have no found HN/Reddit threads about the landing page itself.
